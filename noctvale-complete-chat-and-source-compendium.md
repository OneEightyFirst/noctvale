## Current working identity

- Noctvale is an original grimdark fantasy skirmish wargame.
- The current setting name is Noctvale.
- Earlier concept names included Morvallow, but Noctvale became preferred.

## Combat mechanics explored in chat memory

### Strike Pool system

- Current source doc uses Strike Pool combat resolution.
- Strike Pool = Attacks × Damage.
- Original example: an Iron Sword with Attacks 1 and Damage 4 creates a 4-dice Strike Pool.
- Earlier discussion explored A × D = Strike Pool where A is Attacks and D is Damage.
- The user explored ways to make Attacks and Damage independently meaningful.

### Defense and survivability ideas

- Current source doc uses Defense Dice (Df), where each success blocks one strike.
- Crits cannot be blocked by defense dice.
- Earlier discussion explored subtractive defenses:
  - Luck suppresses Attacks.
  - Armor suppresses Damage.
- Other comparison systems explored:
  - +1 / -1 adjustments based on stat comparisons.
  - Defense dice based on Luck and Might comparisons.
  - Base defense of 1 blue + 1 red plus adjustments from comparisons.

### Colored dice pool experiments

- Explored colored dice pools:
  - Red dice for Might (Mt).
  - Blue dice for Luck (Lk).
- Max dice pool caps tested or discussed: 6 and 8.
- Color-specific blocking concepts:
  - Blue blocks blue.
  - Red blocks red.
  - Two red dice may block one blue die.
- This was considered as an alternative to or evolution of Strike Pool / defense systems.

### To-hit and stat framework explorations

- There was a pivot from a “5+” style system toward “stat + die = threshold” to make higher numbers intuitively better.
- A target of ≥8 was discussed to preserve natural 1s as failures.
- Logic: 1 + 6 should not always hit.
- CR / RC were explored as to-hit skills:
  - CR or CC = close combat skill.
  - RC = ranged combat skill.
- Lk and Mt determined how many dice are rolled in some experiments.
- CC / RC drove accuracy.
- Might (Mt) was explored as a merged Strength / Toughness stat.
- Wounds plus Defense handled survival.
- Defense dice could respond to Lk / Mt comparisons:
  - +1 die for being 1 higher.
  - +2 dice for being 2 higher.
- Beat 7 / ≥8 thresholds were explored.
- Fairness and cognitive load when similar fighters clash was a concern.
- The user repeatedly weighed A × D viability against additive pools.

### Ranged and magic combat notes

- RC is used to hit in ranged combat.
- Ranged Strike Pool may be derived from weapon stats rather than fighter stats.
- Ranged / magic crits should probably be rarer than melee crits.
- One idea: ranged / magic crits on pairs of 6s.
- Sniper designs might reduce the crit requirement to a single die.

### Downed system

- Current source doc uses Downed instead of immediate death at 0 Wounds.
- Downed creates dramatic survivals in otherwise lethal combat.
- Current source doc details Recover, Stunned, Help, and Mercy Kill actions.

# Consolidated Design Takeaways from the Sam Pearson Sources

This section condenses the two Sam Pearson source transcripts into actionable Noctvale-relevant design guidance. The full source text appears later in this file.

## Core parts every wargame needs

Sam Pearson identifies five essential parts of a wargame:

1. Turn sequence.
2. Combat.
3. List building.
4. Scenarios.
5. Setting.

He identifies campaign play as an optional sixth part, though for campaign skirmish games like Noctvale it is effectively central.

## Why this matters for Noctvale

Noctvale should not overbuild lore, factions, equipment, or campaign economies before it has a vertical slice. For a first playable prototype, Noctvale needs:

- A working turn structure.
- A working combat system.
- One scenario.
- Two skeletal warbands.
- Enough setting to make those two warbands and the scenario feel like Noctvale.
- Optional but likely useful: a minimal campaign / exploration stub after the battle.

## Vision document method

The Sam Pearson process:

1. Write a roughly 100-word elevator pitch.
2. Pull out key phrases and expand them into short vision slides / sections.
3. Define design tenets.
4. Rank the tenets.
5. Use the ranked tenets to evaluate mechanics.
6. A lower-ranked tenet may be broken only to serve a higher-ranked tenet.

## Black Death City design tenets from the source

Sam Pearson’s example tenets:

1. The city is a dark and dangerous place.
2. The city hungers for hubris.
3. The law of the sword is simple. Yet its consequences are not.
4. Powder misfires, Sorcery devours.
5. Battles are fought with blood and smoke.

These are also in-world idioms, which lets the vision document double as worldbuilding.

## Design principles relevant to Noctvale

- A wargame’s vision should guide mechanics.
- Mechanics should not be chosen only because they are individually appealing.
- Avoid “Homer’s car”: a pile of liked features without a cohesive vision.
- Short vision documents are better because they stay usable.
- Natural English rules are preferred over excessive jargon.
- Underdog mechanics and negative feedback loops help prevent death spirals and enable dramatic reversals.
- Emergent play creates memorable stories through unexpected but rules-driven events.
- War-as-simulation favors mechanics that feel like a living world, not abstract game currencies.
- Modern twist means avoiding tedious old-school resolution while preserving old-school feel.
- Prefer streamlined inputs with crunchy outputs.
- Avoid crunchy inputs that produce trivial outputs.

## Combat design principles relevant to Noctvale

- Start from desired player experiences rather than dice mechanics.
- Ask what combat should feel like at the table.
- Melee can be the dramatic spotlight of the game.
- Ranged combat can be dangerous without dominating if targeting, cover, and screening rules constrain it.
- Armor should matter.
- Heavy armor should let fighters behave differently, not merely survive longer.
- Duelist and tank archetypes need mechanical space.
- A system can blend influences, but the goal is not to copy; the goal is to evoke the intended experience.
- Randomness should create unresolved tension at decision points, not just chaos.

## Black Death City prototype lessons relevant to Noctvale

- A working prototype is a vertical slice, not a complete game.
- Turn structure and combat need to be nearly complete for a prototype.
- List building, scenarios, and setting only need thin slices.
- A prototype lets you see the whole game in motion before overdeveloping isolated systems.
- Once a prototype works, the game exists and can be polished / expanded.

## Black Death City turn-structure lessons relevant to Noctvale

- Alternating activation fits frantic skirmish games.
- Two unrestricted actions can produce undesirable behavior:
  - Double move can create weird positioning incentives.
  - Double attack can punish charging and encourage static play.
  - Ranged fighters may camp and shoot twice.
- One move plus one action can force movement and attack to both matter.
- Avoid initiative gimmicks if they create design space that violates the game’s vision.
- Surge activations can soften activation-count imbalance.
- Underdog action-economy mechanics can prevent outnumbered warbands from being helpless.

## Black Death City melee system summary

Sam’s melee system includes:

1. Pick target.
2. Pick weapons.
3. Determine initiative.
4. Both fighters roll attack dice.
5. Fighters alternate spending successful attacks to attack or parry.
6. Armor rolls are made against successful attacks.
7. Damage is allocated.

Key elements:

- Charge grants initiative.
- Otherwise, roll and add Initiative.
- Natural 6s are critical hits.
- Regular successes meet or exceed Blade.
- Parries require sacrificing successful attacks.
- Parry fails on 1.
- Critical hits require critical hits to parry.
- Armor adds uncertainty after attack/parry decisions.
- Critical damage may be random.

## Black Death City ranged system summary

Sam’s ranged system is simpler:

1. Pick weapon and target.
2. Make attack rolls.
3. Resolve successful attacks through armor and damage.

Key constraints:

- Target must generally be the closest visible eligible enemy.
- If the closest eligible enemy is in cover, the closest eligible enemy not in cover may be targeted instead.
- This gives defending players agency through screening.
- It lets ranged attacks be dangerous without deciding the game alone.
- A short bow with one shot and D6 critical damage gives a low but real chance to kill a baseline fighter.

## Design tension for Noctvale

Noctvale’s current source doc uses Strike Pool and defense dice rather than Black Death City’s opposed melee duel. The Sam Pearson source suggests a design question for Noctvale:

- Does Noctvale want fast Strike Pool lethality, or does it want melee to be a dramatic back-and-forth duel?
- If keeping Strike Pool, what creates the duelist / tank distinction?
- If adopting opposed melee, what happens to the current weapon and magic critical triangles?
- If keeping defense dice, how does armor always matter without crits bypassing too much?

## Noctvale-specific recommendation implied by all sources

The source material points toward a compact first prototype:

- Keep Noctvale’s current Strike Pool system for the first test unless it fails in dry runs.
- Do not add every faction, spell, injury table, or campaign location yet.
- Build two warbands from the existing faction list.
- Use a single scenario about relic fragments or the castle betrayal.
- Test whether Strike Pool + defense dice + Downed produces the intended lethal-but-dramatic feel.
- Use the Sam Pearson vision method to decide whether future changes serve Noctvale’s desired identity.

THE FALL OF THE REALM

Long before the wars, the lands now called Noctvale were united beneath a single crown. The realm prospered under the rule of Emperor __________, a wise and ambitious ruler who forged alliances between distant kingdoms, built roads across wild lands, and expanded the borders of the empire farther than any ruler before him. Trade flourished, cities grew rich, and the people believed they were living in the dawn of a golden age.

In those years the emperor was advised by scholars of what would later become the Arcane Conclave, protected by the holy warriors who would one day be known as the Phoenix Guard, and served by provincial governors who enforced the law throughout the realm. For a generation the people believed the empire would stand forever.

But power attracts darkness.

During the empire’s greatest expansion, the emperor encountered a mysterious noblewoman who arrived at court from lands beyond the eastern mountains. She called herself Lady __________, claiming descent from an ancient royal lineage long thought lost.

She was beautiful, brilliant, and impossibly persuasive. Within a few short years she rose within the imperial court, becoming the emperor’s closest advisor and eventually his consort.

None realized then that she was something far older than any mortal queen.

Lady __________ was a vampire, one of the ancient predators who had once ruled the night before the rise of human kingdoms. She had watched the empire grow from the shadows and saw in the emperor the perfect vessel through which she could reclaim dominion over the world.

Over time she whispered to him of power, immortality, and the frailty of mortal rule.

Eventually, the emperor accepted her gift.

When the transformation was complete, the empire had already begun to change.

THE SHATTERING

The emperor did not die.

He ascended.

The court soon realized the truth too late. Loyal nobles disappeared in the night. Entire noble houses were replaced by pale-faced courtiers who no longer walked in the daylight. Provinces fell under the rule of the emperor’s new aristocracy.

Word spread quickly across the empire.

The emperor had become a vampire.

Rebellion erupted across the realm. Dukes declared independence. Cities raised militias. Religious orders called for holy war. Witch hunters burned suspected collaborators. In the chaos, ancient creatures long driven into hiding began to emerge from forests, mountains, and forgotten places.

Thus began the conflict remembered as The War of __________, a brutal century-long struggle that would tear the empire apart.

THE HUNDRED YEARS OF WAR

For a hundred years the land burned.

The emperor’s court transformed itself into a new aristocracy of immortal predators. Noble houses were turned into bloodlines of vampires loyal only to the throne.

Against them rose countless factions born from desperation and war.

The Radiant Crusade swore sacred vows to destroy the undead king and his court. The Inquisition declared all magic a corruption that had doomed the empire. The Silver Hunt tracked the monstrous beasts that spread through the wild lands. The Arcane Conclave sought knowledge of the supernatural forces now unleashed. And villages formed watches to protect their homes from all the horrors that roam the land.

Meanwhile darker powers took root in the shadows.

Witches were forced to band together in secret covens to protect themselves from those who believed that all magic—and all who were different—were evil and deserved to be purged.

The Infernal Pact formed secret brotherhoods devoted to demonic powers. The Grave Wardens learned to command the restless dead rising from endless battlefields. And in the wilderness, savage packs of werebeasts formed the warbands now called The Nightpack.

The war became more than rebellion.

It became a struggle for the soul of the world.

THE FALL OF THE VAMPIRE KING

After a century of devastation, the emperor’s reign finally ended.

A coalition led by the Phoenix Guard breached the imperial capital in the final years of the war. There, beneath burning skies and collapsing towers, the vampire emperor was finally destroyed.

But victory came far too late.

The empire had already collapsed. Cities lay in ruins. Provinces had become isolated territories ruled by warlords, cults, or monsters.

Worse still, the emperor’s queen had vanished before the capital fell.

THE AGE OF NOCTVALE

The empire no longer exists.

The land is now known simply as Noctvale.

The ancient aristocracy of vampires survives as The Nobility, scattered across ruined estates and dark fortresses. They no longer attempt to rule the realm as kings.

Instead they indulge in endless decadence.

Bands of vampire nobles roam the countryside in lavish revelries and endless hunting parties, treating the shattered lands as their private playground. Villages are raided for sport. Travelers vanish along lonely roads. Entire regions live in fear of the next wandering courtly procession.

To The Nobility, the world belongs to them.

The rest of the realm is left to fend for itself.

In the absence of the empire’s authority, those who once enforced the law took power for themselves. The organization now known as The Iron Law rose to fill the vacuum, but power without restraint soon corrupted them, and they now rule through fear, chains, and the barrel of a gun.

Across Noctvale, the factions born during the long war still struggle for power, survival, and vengeance.

Some fight to restore order. Some seek forbidden knowledge. Some serve darker masters.

And some simply fight to survive another night.

The empire is gone.

But the war never truly ended.

Actions

Each fighter has 2 actions per activation.

Movement Actions

Move: Move up to movement value.

Charge: Move up to movement value and must end in engagement range (1 inch).

Climb: Move vertically at half movement. Cannot end in engagement.

Scramble: Half movement through difficult terrain. Cannot end in engagement.

Jump: Move up to movement across open space. Must pass a Luck roll. Cannot end in engagement.

Retreat: Attempt to leave engagement. Roll Luck. Failure allows the opponent an opportunity attack if they are not engaged with another enemy.

Combat Actions

Melee: Attack an enemy in engagement range.

Ranged: Attack with a ranged weapon.

Cast: Cast a spell. Spells can be cast while in melee.

Interaction Actions

Interact: Open doors, search objects, pick up items.

Recover: Attempt to stand from Downed.

Help: Assist a stunned ally.

Mercy Kill: Execute a stunned enemy.

Engagement Rules

Engagement range is 1 inch.

No facing system.

Models are treated as aware in all directions.

Line of Sight

Line of sight is drawn model to model. Parts of the miniature extending beyond the base are ignored.Cover

If more than half of the model is obscured, the target has cover. Cover grants +1 Defense Die.

Falling

Roll Luck when falling. For every 2 inches fallen, roll one die. Each 4+ result causes 1 wound.

Multiple Engagement

When attacking multiple enemies:

Attacks may be split among standing enemies.

If one enemy is standing and another is downed or stunned, the standing enemy must be prioritized.

Overwatch

At the start of the round, the player with the fewer number of activations receives a number of overwatch tokens equal to the difference in activations. A player may choose to forgo a model's activation and give that model an overwatch token. The token then allows that model to do a single action as a reaction to an enemy model's completing an action.

Examples: Move, Shoot, Charge, Fight, Retreat Gang Up

If a friendly fighter is also engaged with the same enemy: +1 to the Strike Pool.

Magic System

Spells use 2d6 casting rolls.

Double 1s cause a Mishap.

Every spell has a negative result representing magical backlash.

Each magic class contains at least one ranged damage spell similar to Arcane Bolt.
